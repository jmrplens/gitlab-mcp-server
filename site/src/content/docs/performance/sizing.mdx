<Aside type="tip" title="At a glance">

- **Sizing is two questions, not one.** Holding a credential is nearly free
  (7.7 KiB at rest on the dynamic surface); serving calls is where an instance
  runs out.
- **Pinning `--tier` is a sizing decision.** It removes the tier from the
  configuration shape key and collapses up to three registered catalogs into
  one.
- **`--max-http-clients` is not a memory setting.** Its default of 100 bounds
  about five mebibytes of tenancy.
- **The ceiling is threads divided by processor time per call**, and everything
  past it is queueing rather than a server that got slower.

</Aside>

Everything on this page is HTTP mode. The figures for the reference host, an
Intel i5-14400 with 16 threads, come from the
[resource benchmark](/gitlab-mcp-server/performance/resource-benchmark/), which
measures the real binary and names the machine, the build and the date it ran
on. When it is re-measured its values are replaced wholesale: they are the
measured build's costs, with no comparison against earlier ones. The
per-credential figures at rest come from a second host, named where they are
used. What moved between measurements, and why, is kept in
[Resource Hot Spots](https://github.com/jmrplens/gitlab-mcp-server/blob/main/docs/development/resource-hot-spots.md).

Read [HTTP Server Mode](/gitlab-mcp-server/operations/http-server/) first if the
pool, the instance allow-list and the authentication modes are not already
familiar.

## Two quantities, not one

**Holding a credential is nearly free.** The catalog, its schemas, the discovery
index and the tool manifest are shared per configuration, and the MCP server
itself is shared per configuration **shape**, so what a credential costs is its
GitLab client, its rate-limit bucket, its listen counter, its watchers and its
sessions.
[ADR-0020](https://github.com/jmrplens/gitlab-mcp-server/blob/main/docs/development/adr/adr-0020-one-server-per-configuration-shape.md)
records the design and its invariants.

**Serving a call is where an instance runs out.** Per-call processor time is
the term that binds, and it is untouched by any of that sharing. Everything
below follows from keeping those two apart.

## What a credential costs at rest

Measured at rest with no connection open, a pooled credential costs **7.7 KiB on
the dynamic surface, 8.3 KiB on meta and 8.5 KiB on individual**. It is the same
figure three times over, because what a credential holds is its GitLab client
and its bookkeeping rather than its tools.

Those figures are the shipped regression probe's,
`TestSharedServer_LiveHeapDoesNotGrowWithTheNumberOfCredentials`, which runs on
every push: the median of fifteen runs on an AMD Ryzen 5 3550H (8 threads,
60.8 GiB, Go 1.27.1). Its clients complete a `tools/list` and disconnect, so
what it reads is the pool entry alone, and from one credential to twenty the
live heap grows 0.14 MiB on the dynamic surface and 0.16 MiB on individual.

Three things bound that measurement, and all three matter when you apply it:

- It is the **live heap** after a collection, not the resident set. The resident
  set is larger and does not shrink promptly, because Go returns memory to the
  operating system lazily.
- It is **with no connection open**. A credential holding one also costs the
  buffers behind it, which the resource benchmark measures separately and
  reports as a larger per-credential slope: 30 to 53 KiB of settled live heap,
  with two to four connections open per credential
  ([both figures, reconciled](/gitlab-mcp-server/performance/resource-benchmark/#sizing-a-deployment)).
- It is **at rest**. A credential with requests in flight costs what those
  requests allocate, which is the term that actually dominates. Any
  per-credential figure measured under load mixes tenancy with load and cannot
  be multiplied by a credential count.

## What a configuration shape costs, once

A **shape** is everything that decides what a server registers: tool surface,
capability surface, meta parameter-schema mode, tier and whether it was pinned,
GitLab.com or self-managed, read-only, the narrowing a `read_api` token causes,
safe mode, excluded tools, token scopes, and whether the transport is
stateless. Every credential hashing to one shape is served by one MCP server.

A process holding one registered shape and one credential has a live heap of
about **39 MiB on the dynamic surface, 33 MiB on meta and 88 MiB on
individual**: the concurrency series' first step on the reference host, read
with the load stopped and a collection forced. An HTTP process with no
credential at all, and therefore no shape registered, holds 35 to 38 MiB
resident there.

How many shapes a deployment produces is small and bounded, and you control most
of it:

| Input                | Values in one deployment                                                               |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tier                 | 1 with `--tier` pinned; up to 3 (free, premium, ultimate) when detected per credential |
| Token scopes         | 2, because only `admin_mode` changes the catalog                                       |
| Scope detection      | 2, or 1 with `--ignore-scopes`                                                         |
| `read_api` narrowing | 2, or 1 with `--ignore-scopes`; `--read-only` does not collapse it                     |
| GitLab.com or not    | 1, or 2 for a deployment publishing both                                               |
| Everything else      | 1 each; they are process settings                                                      |

A deployment with `--tier` pinned and `--ignore-scopes` produces exactly one
shape. A typical one produces two to eight, and the worst case is under fifty.
Each costs one catalog build, which the first `tools/list` of that shape pays:
0.32 seconds on the dynamic and meta surfaces and 1.28 on individual on the
reference host, paid once for the process rather than once per credential. A
later credential of the same shape finds it built and waits only for a warm
listing.

This is the consequence worth naming: **pinning `--tier` is a sizing decision,
not only a correctness one.** It removes the tier from the shape key and
collapses up to three catalogs into one.

## What a call costs

On the reference host, under the concurrency series' load of four requests in
flight per credential on the dynamic and meta surfaces and two on individual:

| Surface      | Processor time per call | What dominates it                                  |
| ------------ | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `dynamic`    | about 8 ms              | The SDK's result marshalling and schema validation |
| `meta`       | about 8 ms              | The same                                           |
| `individual` | about 120 ms            | Marshalling a 3 MB `tools/list` response           |

```text
calls per second ceiling  =  usable threads  /  processor seconds per call
```

`16 / 0.008` is 2,000 calls a second in theory. With each credential keeping
four requests in flight, the measured series on the dynamic surface reaches
about 1,370 calls a second at five credentials and plateaus at roughly
**1,700 to 1,800 calls a second** between fifty and two hundred, never
exceeding 1,800 however many more credentials are added; at its top that
plateau is about fourteen of the host's sixteen threads. The individual surface plateaus at about **130 calls a second**
on the same host: every second call of the series is a `tools/list`, which
there marshals 3.2 MB, so an average call costs fifteen times a dynamic one.

Use it to size, not as a promise: your GitLab is across a network this
measurement did not cross.

## The worked example

Five hundred developers, an editor each, on the default dynamic surface, against
one Ultimate self-managed instance, with `--tier=ultimate` pinned.

**Memory.** With the tier pinned, only token scopes and the `read_api` narrowing can
still split the shape (the table above), so one to a few catalogs, and exactly
one with `--ignore-scopes` as well; a process holding one is about 39 MiB of
live heap. Five hundred
credentials at 7.7 KiB is under 4 MiB, and under 25 MiB even at the 50 KiB a
connected one costs. Tenancy is not the term that sizes this deployment; the
peak resident set is decided by how many calls are in flight. Size for the
concurrency, then add a comfortable margin for Go's lazy return of memory to
the operating system. A 512 MiB floor is the right starting point while no more
than about twenty credentials are calling at once, which peaked at 364 MiB in
the benchmark, and the first thing to measure is the peak resident set under
your own traffic rather than the credential count.

**Processor.** Five hundred editors do not make five hundred concurrent calls.
Fifty calls a second is `50 × 0.008 = 0.4` of a thread on the reference host. A
thousand is eight threads, a little over half of one reference host's ceiling; add
a second instance for headroom rather than for memory.

**Reach for a second instance for availability first.** A single instance holds
a large population cheaply. The ceiling that usually binds a busy deployment is
GitLab's own rate limit against one token, which no number of instances raises.

## What counts as a client

The word hides five different things, and only some of them cost anything:

| Term              | What it is                                                                                                                                                          | What it costs the process                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Configured client | Someone has the server in an editor's configuration and has sent nothing                                                                                            | Nothing: no pool entry, no session                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Connected client  | A client that has sent a request. Under the default stateless transport its session ends with each response; under `--stateless=false` it holds an `Mcp-Session-Id` | One pool entry for its token and GitLab URL, shared with every other client of that pair. A session only under `--stateless=false`: four goroutines idle, seven while it holds its standalone stream |
| Active client     | A connected client with requests in flight                                                                                                                          | Its pool entry, plus whatever those requests allocate while they run, which is the term that sizes an instance                                                                                       |
| Idle client       | Connected, with no recent requests                                                                                                                                  | Its session's goroutines under `--stateless=false`, plus one goroutine per resource it watches                                                                                                       |
| Unique token      | A distinct token and GitLab URL pair in the pool                                                                                                                    | One pool entry: a GitLab client, a rate-limit bucket, a listen counter and its watchers, never a tool catalog                                                                                        |

Several clients on one token share its pool entry: one person with three editor
windows on the same token and instance is one entry, and under
`--stateless=false` three sessions on it.

## Measured at scale

Two numbers per row, because holding credentials and serving them are different
costs. The tenancy column is the settled live heap the credentials add,
measured with the load stopped; the load column is the peak resident set with
every credential keeping two to four requests in flight, which is the figure a
container limit has to survive. Both come from the benchmark on the reference
host: the concurrency series for every row but 64, whose peak is the point
scenarios' and whose tenancy is the series' slope applied to it.

| Live credentials | Tenancy, added to the live heap | Peak resident set, all of them calling | Notes                                    |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1                | Nothing measurable              | 146 to 296 MiB                         | One stdio process weighs about the same  |
| 20               | about 1 MiB                     | 257 to 1073 MiB                        | A small team calling at once             |
| 64               | 2 to 3 MiB                      | 324 to 1422 MiB                        | Admitted one at a time, then all calling |
| 100              | 3 to 5 MiB                      | 0.4 to 1.7 GiB                         | The default `--max-http-clients`         |
| 1000             | 29 to 51 MiB                    | 1.0 to 4.6 GiB                         | Every surface's series reached this step |

In the load column the lower figure of each range is `meta` and the higher is
`individual`; in the tenancy column `individual` is the lowest. In practice,
sized by the callers who will be calling **at the same moment**, which is not
the number of people with access:

- **Up to twenty at once**: 512 MiB is enough on `dynamic` or `meta`, where
  twenty credentials all calling peaked at 364 and 257 MiB; allow 1.5 GiB on
  `individual`, which peaked at 1073 MiB.
- **Up to a hundred at once**: 1 GiB on `dynamic` or `meta` and 2 GiB on
  `individual`, against measured peaks of 608, 406 and 1724 MiB. The pool bound
  itself costs about 5 MiB of that.
- **Beyond that**: one process reached a thousand credentials on every surface,
  at 1.0 GiB on `meta`, 1.4 on `dynamic` and 4.6 on `individual` with all of them
  calling. What makes several instances the better shape at this size is
  latency rather than memory, since a saturated host queues: run them behind a
  balancer that routes on a digest of the credential
  ([Load Balancing](/gitlab-mcp-server/enterprise/load-balancing/)), because
  the pool and the OAuth identity cache are per instance and a caller that lands
  on another instance builds its entry again there.

### Where the memory goes

Ranked by how much each grows with the credential count, as the heap profiles
show it at a thousand credentials on `dynamic`:

1. **Requests in flight**: the JSON being decoded and encoded, and the read and
   write buffers of every open connection. The buffers alone were 35.6 MiB of
   live heap across four thousand connections, the largest term that grows at
   all.
2. **Pooled credentials**: credential state only, principally the GitLab client
   at about 5 KiB each. Bounded by `--max-http-clients` and reclaimed by
   `--pool-idle-timeout`, which spares an entry still serving a subscription.
3. **Configuration shapes**: one catalog and one MCP server each, shared by every
   credential of the shape. It does not grow with the credential count:
   `toolutil.cloneSchemaMap`, which builds the catalog's schemas, held 10.0 MiB
   at one credential and 10.0 MiB at a thousand.
4. **Stateful sessions**, under `--stateless=false` only.
5. **Large GitLab responses**, such as long list pages, while they are being
   rendered.

The catalogs are the persistent cost and there are as many of them as there are
shapes, but they are a fixed cost rather than growth. What grows is the
concurrent work, and the pool contributes kilobytes per credential to it.

## The knobs, and what to set them to

| Setting                                   | Default value | What it bounds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | At scale                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `--max-http-clients`                      | `100`         | Pooled credentials, evicted least recently used first; upper bound 10000                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Set it above your expected simultaneous population. Eviction is no longer cheap: it ends that credential's subscriptions and closes its listen streams. Size pressure prefers an entry that is not serving one and takes a busy entry only when every entry is busy, and a busy entry holds one of the two process-wide ceilings (512 listen streams, 512 watchers), so a value above their sum of 1024 makes that fallback unreachable at rest on either transport; 513 suffices on the default stateless one, where a watcher needs a stream. See [the lever](/gitlab-mcp-server/operations/http-server/#the-lever-and-what-it-costs) |
| `--pool-idle-timeout`                     | `1h`          | How long an unused entry is kept                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Leave it. The sweep runs every quarter of the timeout with a one-minute floor, so an entry can outlive the timeout by that much. An entry with live watchers or open listen streams is never idle-evicted: a watcher polls GitLab on its own and a listen is one request the client never repeats, so a subscriber would otherwise look idle                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `--revalidate-interval`                   | `15m`         | How often pooled credentials are re-checked against GitLab                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | The sweep is serial with a ten second timeout per entry, so a large pool against a slow instance costs up to `entries × 10s` per round. Raise it, or accept the lag; an entry older than an hour is rebuilt on next use regardless                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `--rate-limit-rps` / `--rate-limit-burst` | `10` / `40`   | Calls a second per credential that reach GitLab, plus `tools/list` on a bucket of its own refilled a tenth as fast with the same burst, charged first to one bucket for the whole process of 3000 tools a second with 48000 in hand, which holds one credential's whole default burst of listings | Per credential **per process**. Several instances multiply it unless affinity pins each caller to one: see [the limiter multiplies](/gitlab-mcp-server/enterprise/load-balancing/#the-limiter-multiplies). Size the burst for the clients that reconnect together on one credential: each lists once as it connects, and a refused listing is a JSON-RPC error that the client, not a model, has to survive                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `--action-timeout`                        | `65m`         | How long one action may run                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Above the longest wait any action offers. Lower it only if you would rather fail a pipeline wait than hold a goroutine; raise it if callers move files too large to upload or download in that time, since it ends a transfer too                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `GITLAB_MCP_MAX_LISTEN_STREAMS`           | `64`          | Open `subscriptions/listen` streams per credential                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | A second ceiling of 512 per process is deliberately not configurable, because the per-credential one multiplies by however many tokens one caller holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `--session-timeout`                       | `30m`         | Idle MCP session lifetime                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Only under `--stateless=false`; under the default transport a session ends with its POST. A session nobody deletes holds one of the instance's session slots until it expires, and with `0` until the pool evicts its credential, so shorten it where stateful clients walk away without a `DELETE` rather than switching it off                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `--http-idle-timeout`                     | `0`           | Idle connection closure                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Leave it disabled. A positive value cuts long-lived streams                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `--drain-delay`                           | `0`           | How long `/health` answers `503 draining` before the listener closes                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Set it to at least one balancer detection interval. See [the drain window](/gitlab-mcp-server/enterprise/load-balancing/#health-driven-ejection-and-the-drain-window)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

Three further limits are not configurable: ten resource watchers per credential
and 512 across the process, the calls held open at once across the process, and
under `--stateless=false` the sessions the process keeps.

The process watcher ceiling refuses rather than stopping another credential's
watch, because a watcher belongs to one credential and stopping one to admit
another is the trade this server does not make; at its own ceiling of ten, a
credential first gives up its longest-demoted watch to make room and is refused
only when none is demoted. Both refusals carry `-32000`: the process one says
`server-wide` in as many words, and the per-credential one carries that
credential's own count, so a client's error says which number it met. A
credential is one API call to mint, so a per-credential number multiplies by
however many a caller holds and only the process-wide one bounds the process:
52 credentials at their cap of ten are enough to reach it. Holding it is cheap
under `--stateless=false`, where a watcher needs no held connection, and a
deployment whose subscribers crowd each other out this way says so with a
`WARN` once per refusal; there is no number to raise in answer. The same
ceiling bounds the polling an instance sends GitLab: 512 watchers at the
five-second floor are about 102 requests a second leaving the process, and
about 34 at the fifteen-second base
([cadences](/gitlab-mcp-server/capabilities/subscriptions/#how-fast-and-at-what-cost)).

The held-call ceiling follows the
descriptor limit the process runs under rather than a number: each call waiting
on GitLab costs it two file descriptors (the client's connection and the
outbound one), so it leaves an eighth of the limit spare and one descriptor for
each of the 512 listen streams and gives the rest to held calls, two apiece. That
is 192 under a hard limit of 1024 and 229120 under the 524288 a systemd service
gets by default; the hard limit is the one that counts, because the Go runtime
raises the soft limit to it before the server starts. A call past it is refused
as busy, with `503` and a `Retry-After` of 30 seconds on protocol 2026-07-28
([the refusal on each revision](/gitlab-mcp-server/operations/http-server/#requests-held-open-at-once)).
Measured with the benchmark's
[held mode](/gitlab-mcp-server/performance/resource-benchmark/#the-held-mode),
a held call costs two descriptors, six goroutines, about 51 KiB of live heap and
about 190 KiB of resident set; under a hard limit of 1024, with 4000 calls
offered from one credential or from a hundred, the process held 192, refused
the other 3808 and failed none, stayed at 394 descriptors and answered
`/health` in a millisecond. Where the limit is large, memory runs out first, at
about 190 KiB a held call, so the memory limit the process runs under is the
figure to size, a
memory limit on the container or `MemoryMax` on the systemd unit (a unit
without a `MemoryMax` of its own is bounded by the slices above it where one of
them sets one, and otherwise only by the host; `systemctl show -p
EffectiveMemoryMax <unit>` reports the tightest of those limits on systemd 256
and later, and prints nothing on earlier versions): the server keeps no memory
cap of its own, by decision (issue 951). No per-credential ceiling stands beside
it, by the same decision and for the reason given above:
a number on a credential multiplies by however many a caller holds. One
credential can therefore fill it where the limit is small.

The session ceiling is half the held-call one, 96 under a hard limit of 1024 and
114560 under 524288, because each session takes a held-call slot for its
standalone stream when it opens: an idle session costs four goroutines, 10 to
20 KiB of live heap and 88 to 110 KiB of resident memory and holds no
connection, and its stream costs one descriptor and three goroutines more. The
next `initialize` is refused as busy in the same words
([stateful sessions kept at once](/gitlab-mcp-server/operations/http-server/#stateful-sessions-kept-at-once)).
Measured with the benchmark's
[sessions mode](/gitlab-mcp-server/performance/resource-benchmark/#the-sessions-mode)
under a hard limit of 1024, with 4000 sessions offered with their streams, the
process kept 96 in at most 183 descriptors, refused the other 3904 and kept
answering `/health`; before the ceiling it kept every session until the streams
had taken all 1024 descriptors and then stopped answering. Where the limit is
large the ceiling bounds descriptors and not memory: 114560 idle sessions would
take about ten to twelve GiB, so the memory limit the process runs under (a
container's, or a systemd unit's `MemoryMax`) is again the figure to size, and
no fixed cap stands beside the derived one. A session nobody deletes keeps its
slot until `--session-timeout`, and with `--session-timeout=0` until the pool
evicts its credential. One credential can fill the ceiling at no cost, since
`initialize` spends no rate-limit token, and no per-credential ceiling stands
beside it, by the same decision and for the same reason.

The failed-authentication budget is configurable: ten failures a minute per
client address by default (`--auth-failure-limit`, `--auth-failure-window`)
before that address is blocked for the window. What the block refuses is an
authentication rather than an address: a credential the deployment is already
serving is still served from a blocked address, and every credential it does
not already hold is answered `429`
([rejection status codes](/gitlab-mcp-server/operations/http-server/#rejection-status-codes)).
A second budget counts the distinct credentials one address has had refused
and blocks for longer each time
([authentication budgets](/gitlab-mcp-server/operations/security/#authentication-budgets)).
Behind a proxy both have a trap, described in
[Operations at Scale](/gitlab-mcp-server/enterprise/operations/#what-to-monitor).

## What does not scale with the credential count

- **The registered catalog**, paid once per shape.
- **The catalog build**, likewise. A credential arriving at a shape another
  credential already built waits for nothing.
- **Goroutines**, which are per watcher, per open listen stream, per request in
  flight and per live session rather than per credential. A credential doing
  nothing has none.
- **Processor time between requests.** A pooled credential spends none unless it
  holds subscriptions, whose watchers poll GitLab on their own cadence.

| What runs                                                                 | Goroutines |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| The process with one credential admitted (HTTP, the series' first step)   | 18 to 20   |
| A `tools/call` held open while GitLab has not answered                    | 6          |
| A stateful session (`--stateless=false`), idle                            | 4          |
| A stateful session holding its standalone stream                          | 7          |
| A resource one credential watches, however many of its sessions subscribe | 1          |

The concurrency series makes the shape plain: holding four requests in flight
per credential, it counted 4015 goroutines at a thousand credentials on
`dynamic` and `meta`, and 2015 on `individual`, where it holds two. Goroutines
track the requests in flight, not the pool.

Once one instance is not enough, the next question is how callers are
distributed across several:
[Load Balancing](/gitlab-mcp-server/enterprise/load-balancing/).
